Xiaomi 18 Pro Max (Global model) review

This is the Xiaomi 18 Pro Max, and it's  got one of the flashiest camera bumps in   the business. It's actually a whole screen,  a second screen like what you'd see on a   foldable. But on a phone like this, you avoid  the potential drawbacks of the folding tech. But is the rear display actually  that functional and useful or just   a gimmick? And what other interesting  features can be found here? We'll have the answers to all of this and more  in our Xiaomi 18 Pro Max review. Xiaomi seems to be pulling out all the stops  with this new globally released flagship. Not only does the Xiaomi 18 Pro Max pack the newest  top chipset, a huge battery, and fast charging,   it's got a powerful set of cameras, too, with  a couple of 200-megapixel sensors in the mix. Xiaomi is also using cutting-edge  display tech here. And of course,   you have the second display on the back as well. It's that back screen that sets the Xiaomi 18  Pro Max apart as far as looks go. Otherwise, the design is quite reminiscent of an iPhone's,  with a flat back and aluminum frame. The phone   is a bit hefty in hand but sturdy, and  I love this matte metallic finish here. This is the Velvet Red colorway, and  it's quite smooth. The other colors   available include Cosmic Blue, Black, and White. One thing I like about this design is that the 18   Pro Max is totally stable when  resting on a table. No wobbling. And Xiaomi has brought back something we  haven't seen on its flagships in quite a while,   a dedicated shortcut button. You can use it to put  your phone on ring or silent or to access certain AI features. But while you can reassign it between  a few tasks, it's not totally customizable. Under the display sits an  ultrasonic fingerprint reader.   It's fast and easy to enroll,  and it's super responsive, too. And you get IP68-rated ingress protection  here against dust and water submersion. Quite a nice-looking package so far. But now,  let's see what this rear display is all about. This might seem like a new concept, but  Xiaomi first dabbled with it way back   in 2021 with the Xiaomi Mi 11 Ultra,  which we happen to still have around. Then last year, the Xiaomi 17 Pro Max took this  to the next level, but it was exclusive to China.   So, this marks the return of Xiaomi's rear  display concept to international markets. What you get is a 2.9-inch OLED panel integrated  with the rear cameras, which sit within a couple   of cutouts. It's more limited than the  secondary screen on a foldable phone. You can't use your typical apps from here, but  you do have access to a calendar and events and   a timer. It's more like a dedicated always-on  display with extra functionality sprinkled in. It's quite customizable. You can pin text  notes or photos to it or choose from different   wallpaper designs for the panel, including a  few with virtual pets living on the screen. These look cute, but I was  more interested in seeing   if there are any more practical features here. It's useful to be able to check the  time at a glance without flipping the   phone over. And if you like to rest  your phone face down on the desk,   you'll still see your notifications and  calls. You can even answer a call from here. There's also integration with supported apps  for displaying relevant info. For example,   you get track info and controls  for music that's playing. Gemini Live is also available on the rear  display. And another nice use case is   with face-to-face live translation. Each person  gets their own screen with the translated text. But it turns out you can take things even  further. You can create your own apps to   work on the rear screen or download  ones other people have already made. With the GenAI Apps feature, you can use AI to  vibe-code an app or game with a prompt. The AI   will convert your idea into the prompt,  and then it spits out something which   is surprisingly functional, like this  new version of the classic game Snake. And last, but definitely not least, is  this screen's use as a viewfinder for the   cameras. So, you can snap selfies using  the more powerful cameras on the back,   an advantage we're used to  seeing from foldable phones. Also, if you're taking a photo of someone else,   they can see themselves on the second  screen and make their best pose. So, Xiaomi is definitely onto something  with this rear display. It's still no   replacement for the main display, but between  notifications, useful shortcuts, custom apps, and using the main cameras for selfies, Xiaomi's  found some genuinely practical uses for it. But you won't be staring at the back  of your phone all of the time. Let's   talk about the front display, which  actually packs a pretty rare feature. The display itself is a 6.9-inch  OLED with a high resolution and   120 Hz refresh rate. It looks great and  packs a bunch of high-end goodies like   12-bit color depth and support  for several HDR video standards. Because of the LTPO tech, the refresh rate  is extra adaptive, but not by default. It will normally alternate between 120 and 60 Hz. To  get more granular changes to as low as 1 Hz when   idling and potentially save more power,  you can enable this mode in settings. But that's not exactly what I  meant when I mentioned advanced   tech. The Xiaomi 18 Pro Max actually has  a hardware-based privacy display feature,   something we've only seen before  on Samsung's Galaxy S26 Ultra. This acts as a built-in privacy screen  protector. If you're in a public space,   for example, and someone rolls up and  tries to look at your phone from the side,   it becomes much harder for them  to make out what's on your screen. You also get additional controls to block  only certain content from prying eyes,   like specific apps or notifications. There are differences from  the Samsung implementation,   though. There's only one level  of this feature on the Xiaomi,   while the S26 Ultra also has a maximum mode, which  is more aggressive in limiting the viewing angles. Also, we noticed that due to the way they've  done the hardware here, even when the privacy   features are off, the side viewing angles  on the Xiaomi are tighter than the Galaxy. The privacy display also affects  the Galaxy's screen brightness. So,   it begs the question, is that  also the case with the Xiaomi? Surprisingly, in our tests, we found  that maximum brightness in automatic   brightness mode is about the same with privacy  display on and off at roughly 1,100 nits. But with privacy mode, we no longer see a  boost when measuring a small portion of the   screen. We're still at about 1,000 nits  compared to more than 4,000 with it off. So, while maximum automatic  brightness remains unchanged,   the privacy mode takes away the screen's ability  to deliver those much brighter highlights. Another thing worth mentioning is that  while the S26 Ultra also has the help of   an anti-reflective coating to reduce glare,  there's no such coating on the Xiaomi. So, overall, we've got a sharp, bright,  and premium display here with a privacy   feature that works well, but seems  to be slightly behind Samsung's. But it's not only about the visuals.  The Xiaomi 18 Pro Max actually has   three speakers. So, how does the audio compare? Besides a bottom-facing speaker  and front-facing earpiece,   there's also a grille on the  backside above the second display. The setup earned a loudness score of Very Good  on our charts, and the sound quality is great,   with respectable dynamic range  and bass. Let's have a listen. I told you that Xiaomi isn't pulling any punches   here. And they definitely didn't  skimp on the battery life either. The global version of the phone  has a 7,500 mAh silicon-carbon   battery. Pretty respectable. And even more  respectable is how it uses that battery. The Xiaomi 18 Pro Max aced our battery life tests,   earning an overall active use  score of over 23 and a half hours. This is right up there near the  top of the charts and generally   smokes phones like the Galaxy S26  Ultra across all of the metrics. However, this phone's impressive run seems  to come to an end with the charging speed. Supporting this battery is 100 W wired  charging, and you might think with that rating,   you could top up pretty fast. But in our  charging test, a full charge took 74 minutes. The phone did replenish a substantial 60%  of its huge battery in the first half hour,   but charging slows considerably towards the  end. Notably slower than many rivals out there. There's also support for 50 W fast wireless  charging, but only with proprietary chargers.   And while it does attract a magnetic charger,  it's not on the level of an iPhone or Pixel,   as it can hardly hold its own weight. One  of Xiaomi's magnetic cases would help there. And charging isn't the only area where the  Xiaomi 18 Pro Max fell short of expectations. The Xiaomi 18 Pro Max is one of the first  phones to use Qualcomm's new flagship silicon,   the Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen  6. It's built on a 2 nm process and features an updated octa-core CPU  and powerful Adreno 850 GPU. It   also promises better AI processing  and improved efficiency overall. We expected some chart-topping  performance here, but actually,   the benchmarks tell a different story. Xiaomi's flagship doesn't outperform the  previous-generation devices that use the   Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5. In fact, it falls  behind the Galaxy S26 Ultra in several benchmarks. And if the peak performance  was a bit disappointing,   things don't get much better when  we look at sustained performance. In our prolonged stress test, the  CPU performance dipped to around   half and wasn't that stable either,  with plenty of dips and spikes. Throttling in a high-powered phone is  something we're used to seeing and is   unlikely to matter much for everyday use, but  demanding gamers may notice the difference. The interface of the phone is Xiaomi's  new HyperOS 4 running on top of Android   17. According to Xiaomi, it packs a  ton of under-the-hood optimizations   to make the UI experience  faster and more efficient. The aesthetics are fluid and bubble-like.  They remind me a lot of iOS, and you even   have an option in settings to make  your toggles transparent or opaque. There's improved connectivity across devices and  ecosystems, like connecting to Apple products. And the feature set here is actually  quite familiar, including a full suite   of AI-based features which you can check  out in one spot through a dedicated menu. And beyond all the AI features, Xiaomi  has also put plenty of thought into   the shooting experience. It packs some  pro-grade tools within the camera app. These include monitoring options  like focus peaking and a waveform,   and the ability to shoot videos in Log or  Dolby Vision or to shoot photos in RAW format. Plus, there's Central Moment, which is  what Xiaomi calls a set of special filters   that simulate the output of actual  camera lenses through the use of AI. We've already seen how the rear screen  can come in handy for photography,   but that's only half the equation.  What about the cameras themselves? At first glance, you might notice only  two cameras on the back of this phone,   but there's also an ultrawide  which sits below the camera island. The ultrawide camera is a 50-megapixel one,   but the main cam and 3.2x telephoto  zoom both have 200-megapixel sensors. And on top of that, the main cam sensor  uses LOFIC technology. Basically,   when a pixel approaches saturation, there's an  additional capacitor there to store excess charge,   helping preserve highlight detail and  extending the sensor's dynamic range. But does all that translate into better quality?  Let's see just how competitive these cameras are. With the default Leica Vibrant profile enabled,  daylight shots from the main camera come out   great. The colors aren't that vibrant,  but they're well-saturated and likable. Dynamic range is excellent, even to the point of a   bit of a flat look. A little  more contrast wouldn't hurt. And the photos are packed with detail,  with quite natural-looking processing. People shots are also very nice. Skin tones  are accurate. Facial detail is rendered well,   and there's a subtle natural background blur. Of course, you can go for the dedicated portrait  mode if you want to lean more into that. The 2x zoom photos are properly great as well,  rendering a ton of intricate detail in a very   mature and likable way. The quality is even  comparable to a dedicated 2x zoom camera. But it's still worth it to have the 3.2x telephoto  cam. Its photos capture excellent detail,   and that's rendered quite nicely, too. Dynamic  range is great, and the colors are easy to like. The portraits on the telephoto  are also hard to fault. With the help of the digital crop, you get to  6.4x zoom. And these photos are also pretty great. The telephoto cam can also focus reasonably close,   so it's useful to take close-ups with. They  come out great, both at 3.2x and 6.4x zoom. While the ultrawide may be lower-res, it still  does a nice job. Photos are sharp and detailed,   and colors and dynamic range are excellent. We also appreciate the autofocus. The field of  view isn't as wide as on competitors, though. In the dark, the 18 Pro Max's main camera  takes properly great photos at its native   focal length. Detail is excellent and  is well-defined and rendered naturally. Highlights are expertly preserved,  and shadows have very good detail,   too. And white balance is on point,  while colors maintain great saturation. The nighttime 2x zoom is excellent, too,   maintaining sharp detail better  than key competitors can manage. And the telephoto camera captures great  shots in the dark at 3.2x zoom. Dynamic   range is excellent. Color rendition  is on point, and detail is very good,   even excellent sometimes, depending on  how much light you have to work with. And at night, the ultrawide maintains  excellent dynamic range and vibrant colors,   but sharpness is more in the so-so category. Onto selfies, and the 18 Pro Max's  front-facing camera takes very   good photos. Colors are a little  restrained, but accurate overall. But since you have the ability to  take selfies with the rear cameras,   you'd probably rather stick to those. The main camera selfies are sharper  than what you get with the selfie cam,   and they have some nice natural background blur. You have the option to zoom, and a 2x  zoom still gets you top-class images. And it's nice to have the option for an ultrawide   selfie to fit a group into the photo  or just see more of the background. So far, it's been a great showing  for the Xiaomi 18 Pro Max when it   comes to still photos. But  how is the video recording? The phone can record videos in up to 8K at 30 fps  with the main and telephoto cameras, and those can   also do 4K at 120 fps slow-mo. The ultrawide  and selfie cameras max out at 4K at 60 fps. Detail is great from the main camera  at 1x and even at 2x digital zoom.   The telephoto's footage is very nice, too. Just the ultrawide comes out a bit softer  than we'd like, but across the board,   the footage has wide dynamic range, great  contrast, and somewhat restrained colors. At night, the main camera is again very  good, and the telephoto cam does a good job. Again, the ultrawide is lagging behind somewhat,   but the footage has wide dynamic range,  accurate white balance, and pleasing colors. So, there you have it. The Xiaomi 18  Pro Max is a great flagship all around,   with not only excellent battery life and great  overall camera quality, but a bunch of unique features as well, like that rear display,  which looks cool and is far from a gimmick. It's actually quite useful in quite a  few different scenarios. And the privacy   display is nice to have, though it's not  quite as advanced as Samsung's version. As far as downsides go, charging isn't  particularly fast, and the chipset falls short   of its full potential. It's merely on par with  most flagships that came out earlier this year. And last but not least is the price tag. This is   really one of the more expensive  phones on the market right now. Still, there's a whole lot here to like, and if  you can stomach the price tag, the Xiaomi 18 Pro   Max is one of the more unique smartphones  out there, and it's worth recommending.
