Google Pixel 11 vs. Pixel 11 Pro

If you're after a new Pixel phone, you  might be wondering which is the better deal:   the vanilla Google Pixel 11 or the  higher-tier Pixel 11 Pro. Of course, the Pro model does come with a few extra features,  but are the differences enough to be worth paying   extra for? We've run both phones through our  gauntlet of tests, and we have the answer. Both the Pixel 11 and Pixel 11 Pro  are compact high-end phones that offer   Google's signature software and camera  experience. But as the vanilla model, the Pixel 11 is about 200 bucks cheaper. The  trade-off is that the Pixel 11's cameras are   a lower grade than the Pro's, and the  display tech isn't as advanced either. The design is about the same, though, with the  signature bar-shaped camera island. However, the Pixel 11 has a glossy back, while the Pixel 11  Pro has a frosted back panel. The inverse is true   for the frames, and the Pixel 11's matte frame  is slightly grippier than the Pro's glossy one. And Google offers different color  options for the Pixel 11 and the   Pixel 11 Pro if those are important to  you. I prefer the Pixel 11's matte look,   but how about you? Let me know which design  you like better down in the comments. Now, there is another difference worth  mentioning. The Pixel 11 is the lighter   of the two, though by less than 10  g, so it's hardly a deciding factor. You also don't get a flashing LED on the back  of the Pixel 11 like you do on the Pro models.   But that's more of a gimmick than a useful  feature, so I'm not even going to count it. Both phones have an IP68 rating with  protection against dust and water submersion. But let's talk a bit about the  displays here. That's one of   the biggest differences between  these phones, at least on paper.   The Pixel 11 has a 6.3-inch OLED with a  1080p resolution and a 120 Hz refresh rate. The Pro model also has a 6.3-inch screen, but  it has LTPO tech for a more adaptive refresh   rate as well as a higher resolution.  This means that the Pixel 11 Pro has a higher pixel density of nearly 500 ppi,  and the picture is technically sharper,   but I don't think you're really able to  notice this difference with the naked eye. Now, there is a difference we were able to  measure, and that's the max brightness. It's   higher on the Pro model, but both the Pixel  11 and Pixel 11 Pro were able to boost to well over 2,000 nits in automatic brightness mode.  That's plenty for comfy use outdoors either way,   but Google still hasn't added an anti-reflective  coating like you see on the iPhones. Under the display of either Pixel  sits an ultrasonic fingerprint   reader for biometrics, and they're just as snappy. But on the vanilla Pixel 11, the refresh  rate isn't as adaptive as you don't have   the LTPO tech. This means that while its high  refresh rate can dial down when the phone idles to save energy, it can't go  down below 60 Hz. The Pro model,   on the other hand, can go down as low as  1 Hz, theoretically saving more energy. So, how did that affect the battery life here?  The Pixel 11 is powered by a 4,985 mAh battery,   while the Pro's is a bit smaller at 4,850 mAh.  And both use the same chipset and software. It turns out that despite the different  battery capacities and screens,   the Pixel 11 and Pixel 11 Pro earned  identical battery life scores in our testing. That's for both the overall active  use score and the individual tests. So,   the LTPO screen efficiency and all that  didn't make much of a difference in the end. And wired charging is rated the  same between these two phones,   30 W max either way. In our charging test,  the Pixels charged at exactly the same rate,   reaching the same mark at 30 minutes and  taking about 80 minutes for a full charge. And both phones support the same  25 W wireless charging. Either way,   that's Qi2 magnetic wireless  charging like the iPhone's MagSafe. As I briefly mentioned earlier,  the Pixel 11 and Pixel 11 Pro have   the same chipset. It's Google's new  Tensor G6 paired with 12 gigs of RAM. The Tensors aren't known for their high  performance, but the G6 does promise better   CPU and AI processing power than last year's  models. It also includes a GPU architecture which doesn't support ray tracing. And at launch, it  wasn't well supported by Android game developers. But despite earlier reports of glitching  and incompatibility when gaming on the   Pixel series, since then the problems  have mostly been ironed out by Google. As expected, in benchmarks, both of  these phones earn the same scores.   It's far from top flagship performance when it  comes to graphics, but CPU-wise, it's still solid. There is no difference in the sustained  performance either. Both Pixels did   a decent job in our prolonged stress tests,  displaying some throttling, but nothing drastic. But when it comes to onboard storage, the Pro  model has the option to come with a terabyte   on board. That's on top of the 256 or 512 GB  options also available on the vanilla model. You'll get Google's software  experience either way,   a clean yet customized version of Android  17, and it runs perfectly smoothly here. Let's touch upon something else, the speakers.  Both phones are just as loud, achieving a score   of Good on our charts, and the sound quality is  about identical. Let's have a listen to compare. But what isn't identical is  the camera setup. It's one of   the other main differences between these phones. On the Pixel 11, you get three cameras  on the back: a main cam, a 5x zoom,   and a fixed-focus ultrawide. The Pro  model's setup is similar, except that its rear cameras have larger, higher-res  sensors, and the ultrawide has autofocus. So, we expect higher quality from the Pro model,   but let's compare some photos  and see the differences. During the day, both phones' main  cameras do a good job with similar   processing typical of the Pixels. The  11 Pro does have the edge in sharpness.   It's not a huge difference, but  it's there and it's consistent. At 2x digital zoom, actually neither  phone does a very impressive job. With the 5x telephoto cam, the Pixel 11 captures  okay shots, but those from the 11 Pro are sharper   and have better-rendered fine detail. A  clear victory for the higher-tier model. Photos from the ultrawide cameras  are actually quite comparable,   at least when shooting faraway subjects.  But only the Pro model is able to focus   up close. In those situations,  it'll get you sharp results. When shooting photos in the dark, the Pixel 11  Pro's main camera will get you better detail   than the Pixel 11. But the Pixel 11 did  prove more reliable in its white balance. At 5x zoom, the 11 Pro again has the upper hand  in terms of sharpness and detail. But yet again,   it's a bit more inconsistent with  its white balance for some reason. At night, the Pixel 11's ultrawide  leaves a lot of noise unchecked,   but its shots are still quite sharp, which  isn't the case with the 11 Pro's images.   So, surprisingly, the vanilla  model is doing better here. The Pixel 11's selfie cam is 10.5 megapixels,  while the Pro's is 42 megapixels. Both have   autofocus and take nice selfies. It's just  that the Pro model has a wider field of view. And here's a look at 4K selfie  videos taken with either phone. Both phones have the same video recording  capabilities on board, except that the vanilla   model's ultrawide maxes out at 4K at 30 fps,  while the Pro model's ultrawide can do 4K at 60. In daytime scenarios, you'll end up  with very similar video quality from   the two phones. We give the edge to the  Pixel 11 Pro. It has a slight sharpness   advantage on all three cameras, but  the Pixel 11 is doing just fine. And both phones are quite close in properties  like the colors, contrast, and dynamic range. At night, when shooting with the  main camera and the telephoto,   the Pro has a small advantage in sharpness. On  the ultrawide, quality is more or less the same. So, there you have it, guys. Google's Pixel  11 versus the more premium Pixel 11 Pro. With either phone, you get a compact  and well-built phone with ingress   protection. The displays are smooth and  very bright and look just as sharp when using them. There's the same chipset and  equivalent gaming performance either way.   The interface and software  experience are just the same. And we got the same results in our speaker  test, as well as our battery life test,   surprisingly. And the charging  is neck and neck as well. Plus,   the overall camera experience  is very similar between the two. The Pixel 11 has some nice advantages in  itself. Its matte frame is a bit grippier.   And this phone provides much the same user  experience as the Pro model at a lower price. The Pixel 11 Pro didn't seem to bring huge  advantages over the vanilla model. Its   display is higher-res and LTPO, but those didn't  affect the user experience at all. The phone also offers an optional 1 TB storage tier if you need  that. And the cameras have a slight edge overall,   with autofocus on the ultrawide cam  and sharper output in many scenarios. In the end, the Pixel 11 can do basically  everything the Pro model can for about 200 bucks   cheaper. If having that bit of extra sharpness  in your photos is what you're after, it could   make sense to go for the Pro. But otherwise,  the vanilla Pixel 11 is the better value.
