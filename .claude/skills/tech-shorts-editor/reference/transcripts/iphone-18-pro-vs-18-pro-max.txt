Apple iPhone 18 Pro vs. 18 Pro Max: Watch This Before You Buy

Okay, you've decided to get the latest  iPhone, but should you go for the more   compact iPhone 18 Pro or the bigger  but more expensive iPhone 18 Pro Max? The Pro Max is the better phone on paper,  but the smaller iPhone 18 Pro offers almost   exactly the same experience for less money.  We put both through our suite of tests,   and for some people the smaller Pro is the  smarter choice. Let me walk you through it. The iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max are  Apple's 2026 flagships, and they offer the   same top-tier features. The Pro is at least  100 bucks cheaper than the Pro Max, though. For that extra cash, the Pro Max offers  a bigger battery, faster charging,   a bigger heat sink, and a larger screen  than what you'd get with the Pro model.   That doesn't make it a clear-cut winner, though. The Pro Max is way heavier, 38 g to be  precise, and in hand it's noticeably bulkier.   Many people would want to choose the Pro  simply because it's more comfy to use. Besides that, the looks and build materials  are the same between the two phones,   and you get the same color options either way. Both of these iPhones have hardware  controls in the way of an Action key   and a dedicated Camera Control key.  There's also equivalent ingress protection,   IP68-rated against dust and water  submersion to a depth of 6 m. But let's compare these two phones' displays. It's  actually one reason you might choose the Pro Max. The iPhone 18 Pro has a 6.3-inch OLED with  a high resolution and high refresh rate.   But the Pro Max is considerably  larger at 6.9 inches. This means that the Pro Max has  notably more screen real estate,   so a bigger picture when watching  things on the phone. And on the Pro Max,   the cutout for the selfie cam takes  up less of the total screen space. At least either way, Apple has reduced the  size of this cutout by moving the IR camera   to under the screen. And Face ID works the same on  either device, as responsive and snappy as ever. When we measure the maximum brightness, these  two phones are neck and neck and very bright.   Both of these displays have  an anti-reflective coating,   too. So, the usability outdoors  in the sun is just as great. Now, we'll move on to the speakers, and  the Pro Max is louder, earning a score of   Very Good on our charts versus Good on the 18  Pro. We'd say the Pro Max sounds better, too,   with more pronounced bass. You can listen for  yourself through our comparison tool linked below. But an even bigger and arguably more  important consideration between these   two phones is the battery life. When comparing  the SIM-enabled versions, the iPhone 18 Pro's battery capacity is a bit over 4,000 mAh,  while the Pro Max's is nearly 5,400 mAh. That's a pretty sizable difference, but the  Pro Max also has a bigger screen to power. So,   let's see how it translates into battery life. In our suite of battery life tests,  the iPhone 18 Pro Max came out ahead,   earning an overall active use score of over 19  hours, while the 18 Pro's was less than 17 hours. Not surprisingly, the Pro Max had its biggest lead  in the 4G call test, where the screen stays off,   but it was comfortably ahead in  all other categories as well. Apple has also given the Pro Max faster charging.  It's 54 W versus 45 W on the iPhone 18 Pro. In our charging test, the difference wasn't huge,  but it was noticeable. The Pro Max charged a few   percentage points more in a half hour, and a  full charge was a few minutes faster as well. But both phones feature the same  25 W Qi2 MagSafe wireless charging. And Apple has equipped both of these iPhones  with the same chipset, the new Apple A20 Pro.   It promises a considerable jump in  performance across the board compared   to the previous generation. And both phones  run high-end games without breaking a sweat. In benchmarks, we indeed see a nice  improvement compared to last year's iPhones.   But between the Pro and Pro  Max, the performance is equal. What isn't so equal, however, is the  thermal management. As I said earlier,   the Pro Max has a larger heat  sink. In our prolonged stress test, the 18 Pro displayed notably more  thermal throttling than the Pro Max.   Not sure how much of an impact that would  really make for your daily use, though. Both devices offer the same storage tiers,   starting from 256 gigs and  going up to 2 terabytes. And there's the same software experience  on both of these iPhones, as expected.   It's iOS 27, with Apple's latest  aesthetics and features on board. And now onto the cameras. Unlike  what we saw a few generations back,   Apple equipped both the new Pro  and Pro Max with the same setup. On the back of either phone, you get a main cam,   a 4x telephoto zoom, and an  ultrawide, all at 48 megapixels. And these main cameras introduce something new  when it comes to Apple, a variable aperture lens.   This lens allows you to manipulate the  depth of field behind a close-up subject.   Adjusting the aperture doesn't seem to  otherwise affect the photo quality, though. Speaking of that quality, it's  identical between these two iPhones,   as you'd expect from two devices with the  same hardware, software, and image processor. From the main cameras, you get  output with conservative colors,   great dynamic range, and very good detail. At 2x zoom, the main camera  still delivers very good results. The 4x telephoto cameras capture good  shots, but it's hardly exciting stuff   if you compare them to proper top-tier  camera phones in the Android world. Similarly, the ultrawides will get you  identical results whether you go Pro or Pro Max.   There's hardly anything to  separate the two phones. That parity naturally continues into  the dark, with both phones capturing   the same low-light photos. The main cameras  produce slightly grainy but detailed shots,   with good dynamic range and  accurate colors from both models. The telephoto images are good for the  most part, though some more aggressive   low-light processing probably wouldn't  hurt. It's the same on either phone. And both the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max  will capture the same ultrawide photos,   with largely underwhelming quality. The selfies are yet another area where you'd  expect the exact same output from both phones.   And here are 4K selfie videos  taken with either phone. Both iPhones have the same wide-ranging video  recording capabilities, including shooting in   ProRes and Apple Log, too. Video quality is,  of course, the same between the two phones, with well-matched output from all three cameras,  great global properties, and very good detail. In the dark, main camera clips are very good,  and so are the ones from the telephoto camera.   But you get the visible internal  reflections usual for iPhones.   The ultrawide quality is just okay, but  without those reflections, at least. So, there you have it, guys. The iPhone 18 Pro  versus its bigger brother, the iPhone 18 Pro Max. Either way, you get a well-built iPhone  with the same looks and premium materials.   The displays are smooth, sharp, and  bright with an anti-reflective coating. There's the same chipset and processing  power on both phones. The Apple interface   and software features are just the same. And  the results from the cameras are identical, too. The iPhone 18 Pro, however, is more compact  and much lighter, so it's easier to wield.   The overall user experience is nearly the same,   and while the phone is far from cheap,  it's a bit less expensive than the Pro Max. On the other hand, the Pro Max does have a few  advantages, including its larger screen, louder   and better-sounding speakers, better thermals,  longer battery life, and slightly faster charging. When you add those all up, they  do amount to a noticeable upgrade,   and it could be worth it to pay the  extra 100 bucks and get the Pro Max. But if you prefer the compact form factor, the Pro  is also a great phone that should serve you well.   And as a bonus, you'd save a bit of cash.
