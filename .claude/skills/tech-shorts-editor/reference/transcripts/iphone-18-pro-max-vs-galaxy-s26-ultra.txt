Apple iPhone 18 Pro Max vs. Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra

Hey, what's up, guys? Today we've got a couple of  the biggest names in the smartphone scene. There's Apple's new iPhone 18 Pro Max versus Samsung's  Galaxy S26 Ultra. It's a clash of the titans. But before you release that Kraken, you won't want  to miss this full comparison. Let's dive into it. The Pro Max and S26 Ultra are basically the top  phones you can get from either camp, and the   most expensive besides the foldables. And as such,  they have to meet some pretty high expectations. Neither purchase is for the faint  of heart or the weak of hand,   as they're far from compact. But the iPhone is  actually much heftier by a long shot, about 35 g. The premium build materials are comparable,  though. They're both made of high-end reinforced   glass and aluminum. Both smartphones are  IP68-rated against dust and water submersion, too. But as we've seen before, the iPhone  is rated to survive in deeper water,   up to 6 m versus 1.5 m on the Galaxy. The iPhone also has a couple of extra hardware  buttons. One is the Action key for shortcuts.   And the other is a Camera Control key,  which can launch the cameras and shoot. These two phones have the same  size display at 6.9 inches.   The Pro Max has an odd resolution, though,  which amounts to a pixel density of 460 ppi. Meanwhile, the Galaxy has a higher QHD resolution.  Both phones have an adaptive 120 Hz refresh rate. The Galaxy's higher resolution at 500 ppi does  look better on paper. But when just watching with   the naked eye, I can't tell the difference.  Both phones look plenty sharp and premium. The Samsung does have a smaller  cutout for the selfie cam, though,   and that's even after Apple trimmed  theirs this year. The iPhone still uses   the selfie camera hardware to enable Face  ID, the only biometric input on the phone. While the S26 Ultra can make use of an  under-display ultrasonic fingerprint reader   for biometrics, it has face unlock, too, but  that isn't as secure as the one on the iPhone   since the Galaxy doesn't have the ToF sensor to  make sure it's not just seeing a photo of you. Apple also uses the space around its  selfie cutout to display shortcuts and   things like timers and playing media. You  won't see that on the Galaxy, but the S26   Ultra has tricks of its own, like a built-in  stylus you can use for writing and drawing. Another exclusive feature is the Privacy  Display. When enabled, it acts like   one of those privacy screen protectors,  keeping your screen safe from prying eyes.   You can disable it at any time or set it  for only certain things like passwords. And despite having a differently built  display to enable that privacy feature,   the S26 Ultra has great max brightness. In fact, the peak brightness we measured is comparable  between these two phones. And not only that,   both the Galaxy and the iPhone have an  anti-reflective coating which helps reduce glare. Put that together and both of these phones have  some of the best sunlight legibility around. As far as audio goes, it's about  a toss-up. Both phones have stereo   speakers which earn the same score  of Very Good on our loudness charts,   and the sound quality is excellent.  Let's have a listen to compare. These two phones offer the same lower storage  options, but the Galaxy caps out at 1 TB, while the iPhone goes up to two. However, the 1  and 2 TB versions of the iPhone use QLC storage,   which has been shown to slow  down after getting over 60% full. Of course, coming from Apple and Samsung,  these two phones have very different chipsets.   The iPhone 18 Pro Max comes with  Apple's newest A20 Pro chipset,   which promises a major boost  in performance over last year. While on the S26 Ultra, you get Qualcomm's current   top chipset for Android flagships.  It's a Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5,   and it's even an enhanced version  for Galaxy with higher clock speeds. In benchmarks, while the Galaxy puts up a good  fight, the iPhone's new hardware edges it out in   both raw CPU and GPU performance. The difference  doesn't amount to much more than bragging rights,   though. With either phone, you get enough  power to handle whatever you can throw at it. It is worth a mention that the iPhone  did do better in our thermal management   stress test as well, though. Apple  boasts a new passive cooling system   this year with a large vapor chamber,  and perhaps that made the difference. Of course, these two phones  use very different software.   The iPhone runs Apple's latest iOS 27 with  this updated Liquid Glass design language. The S26 Ultra runs instead on Android 17  with Samsung's latest One UI 9 on top. Both offer plenty of AI-powered features,  including an onboard AI assistant.   But with the iPhone, some of the AI features  are not available yet in places like Europe. Let's move on to battery life. And this  year's Pro Max actually has the larger   cell. It's 5,391 mAh on the SIM-enabled  version versus 5,000 on the S26 Ultra. The 18 Pro Max is the clear winner in our  battery life tests, earning an active use   score north of 19 hours, while the Galaxy's was  16 and change. And the iPhone did better across   all the individual tests as well, including  web browsing, video streaming, and gaming. So, the iPhone has the better battery  life, but what about the charging speed?   The Pro Max is capable of 54-watt wired  charging versus 60 watts on the S26 Ultra. In our charging test, the two phones are  about neck and neck up to the 30-minute mark,   reaching a little over 80%  in that time. After that,   the Galaxy goes ahead and reaches a  full charge about 10 minutes faster. Both phones support 25-watt Qi2 wireless charging,  but only the iPhone has the built-in magnets.   You need to buy a magnetic case  to enable that for the Galaxy. Onto the cameras, and the 18 Pro Max's  main cam has a new variable aperture lens.   With this, you can control the depth of field  behind close-up subjects. And the rear setup overall includes three cameras: the main cam, a 4x  zoom, and an ultrawide cam, all at 48 megapixels. The S26 Ultra has four cameras on the back,   a 200-megapixel main cam, plus a 3x  zoom, a 5x zoom, and an ultrawide. During the day, both phones' main cameras  will get you excellent daytime shots.   The iPhone defaults to 24 megapixels,   and it might resolve a little more detail,  but the difference is hardly dramatic. The iPhone also leans toward warmer colors,   while the Galaxy's colors have  a bit of extra saturation. At 2x digital zoom, the iPhone is superior.  It renders detail more naturally here.   The Galaxy's 3x zoom camera  is only decent at best,   but it's probably of some use if you want  to bridge the gap between 2x and 5x zoom. Comparing the iPhone's 4x zoom camera  and the Galaxy's 5x one is tricky   because of the different focal length  and resolution, but the Galaxy shots   are more likable. The iPhone's detail  isn't stellar here when you look close. When zooming to twice as far  with the help of a digital crop,   the Galaxy does much better as  long as the lighting is good. The ultrawides come out with photos  with essentially the same detail levels,   even though the iPhone insists on using  twice as many megapixels for its take. At night, neither phone is keen  on using its night mode processing   unless the scene is extremely  dark. In general, in auto mode,   both the iPhone's and the Galaxy's main  cameras will get you good low-light photos. The iPhone's default results tend to be  sharper and more detailed than the Galaxy's,   though it's not a huge difference. The Galaxy's 3x telephoto  isn't amazing in the dark,   capturing soft and gloomy  images in full auto mode. Comparing the 4x and 5x telephotos,   we'd argue that the Galaxy tends to have  an advantage in sharpness and definition. And on the ultrawide end, we're  liking the Galaxy's exposures better.   The iPhone's images are pretty dim. Also,  the Galaxy's rendition is somewhat superior. When shooting selfies, the Galaxy has  some of the best detail rendition around.   The iPhone isn't as great as the  Galaxy in terms of absolute quality,   but it's still pretty good, and  the field of view is wider, too. Plus, the iPhone selfie cam  has a multi-aspect sensor that   allows you to switch orientation  without needing to turn the phone.   And here's a look at the 4K selfie  videos taken with either device. Both phones have a wide range of capabilities  for video recording, including pro-grade codecs   and recording to external storage, but  only on the Galaxy can you record in 8K. Samsung has also made a big deal about  the Horizon Lock mode for stabilization,   where the phone will shoot a mostly level video  regardless of the orientation of the phone. As far as quality goes, the iPhone's footage  is sharper than the Galaxy's across all of   its cameras during the day. However,  when it comes to the other properties   like dynamic range and colors, the  Galaxy does an equally great job. And at night, the iPhone's main and telephoto  again do a bit better in terms of noise and   sharpness. However, when shooting with them,  the iPhone also features prominent internal   reflections, and the ultrawide footage  is better exposed than the Galaxy's. So, there you have it, guys. The iPhone  18 Pro Max and the Galaxy S26 Ultra. Let's do a quick recap of what you'd get  on either phone. Both of these flagships   are large and built from premium materials with  good ingress protection. And they have a large, bright, anti-reflective display. And either  way, there's a high-performance chipset,   advanced AI features, and  a versatile set of cameras. The iPhone, of course, has the iOS software  and Apple ecosystem. The phone has longer   battery life, and there are built-in magnets  for MagSafe support. There are additional controls in the Action key and Camera Control  key. There's advanced Face ID for biometrics.   The video recording quality is better, and  you have the multi-aspect feature for selfies. The Galaxy, on the other hand, has  the benefits that come with being an   Android phone and no region lock on AI features.   It is a bit lighter, and you get the  Privacy Display and stylus support.   There's an ultrasonic fingerprint reader for  biometrics, and selfies come out looking better. In the end, it's quite a toss-up between these  two phones. The iPhone has the advantage in   the better battery life and the nicer  video recording, but there are plenty   of perks to getting the S26 Ultra as well,  including the Privacy Display and the stylus. Either way, you'll get a great flagship.
