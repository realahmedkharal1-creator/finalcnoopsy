Google Pixel 11 Pro XL vs. Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra

Today, we've got two of Android's top smartphones  duking it out. The Pixel 11 Pro XL is Google's latest and greatest flagship. But there's  a problem. Depending on where you shop,   it can actually cost more than  some heavy-hitting Android rivals. Case in point, Samsung's Galaxy S26 Ultra, which  also offers what looks like better specs and a handful of unique features. So, why would anyone  pay extra for Google's flagship? Maybe there's   something the specs don't tell us. We put both  phones through our usual tests to find out. We'll kick things off with the design.  These two are rather different-looking,   at least when it comes to  the camera bumps on the back. The aluminum frame on the Pixel also has a  glossy look compared to the Galaxy's matte   one. While both phones are big, the  Galaxy is the slightly larger device,   but surprisingly, it's actually a bit  lighter than the Pixel by over 10 g. The Pixel does add an aesthetic feature called  Highlight, where LEDs in the camera flash would   glow for certain very limited scenarios,  like when interacting with Gemini. It's   not a terribly useful feature, so  we're not even going to count it. And the two phones have different color  options available. The Pixel can come in   a reddish or green color, while only on the  Galaxy can you get a blue or purple finish. The S26 Ultra does bring more premium  glass, though. On the backside,   Gorilla Glass Victus 2 is used on both devices,  and the Pixel also uses that on the front, while the Galaxy screen is protected by  the more durable Gorilla Armor 2 glass. But at least when it comes to ingress protection,   the two devices are just as reliable, rated  IP68 against dust and submersion in water. Let's talk a bit more about these displays,   though. This is one of the major  differences between these two devices. The Pixel has the slightly smaller screen at 6.8   inches, but it's still quite large  and packs respectable specs. But the   S26 Ultra is 6.9 inches, and there is  a negligibly higher resolution as well. While you wouldn't be able to see a  difference in sharpness with the naked eye,   you might be able to appreciate the  larger viewing area on the bigger Galaxy. But the differences don't stop at the size.  The S26 Ultra has the Privacy Display feature,   which reduces the visibility  of your screen from the sides,   making it harder for people  nearby to see what you're doing. However, Privacy Display does reduce the   S26 Ultra's max brightness  considerably when activated. And speaking of brightness, it's an  interesting comparison. In our test,   the Pixel reached a considerably higher  max auto brightness than the Galaxy,   even with Samsung's Privacy Display switched off. But even though this higher brightness  makes for good legibility outdoors,   the S26 Ultra has one other trick up its  sleeve. It's equipped with an anti-glare   coating to cut those reflections down.  That's something you don't have on the Pixel. And yet another check mark on the  S26 Ultra's list is stylus support,   which adds a bunch of extra functionality  that's hard to find elsewhere. At least for biometrics,   the ultrasonic under-display fingerprint  reader is just as responsive either way. So, besides the brightness, it looks like  the Galaxy comes out on top as far as   the display goes. But can it maintain  the advantage when it comes to audio? Well, not quite. Actually, both phones earned  a Very Good rating in our loudness test,   and the sound quality is great either  way. The tuning is different, though,   with the Pixel offering better highs  and the Galaxy doing better with lows. You can listen for yourself with  our comparison tool linked below. But there is one other aspect in which  the S26 Ultra is a surefire winner,   the chipset performance. This one is a bit of a no-brainer. Google's Tensor   chips have never been known  for their high performance,   but Samsung equips its flagships with the best  Android chips on the market year after year. The Pixel 11 Pro XL runs on the Tensor  G6, which may not be a powerhouse,   but promises good efficiency, as well as better  CPU and AI processing compared to previous models. Meanwhile, the S26 Ultra is equipped with a  top Qualcomm chipset at the time of its launch,   a Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5. And not only that,   it's a special version for Galaxy  with higher clock speeds than usual. As expected, in benchmarks, the Galaxy  S26 Ultra outscores the Pixel 11 Pro   XL by a huge margin. The biggest  gaps were in graphics-based tests,   as graphics have never been a  strong suit of Tensor chips. In fact, this year's Pixels are at even more of  a disadvantage than usual when it comes to gaming   performance. The Tensor G6 has a GPU architecture  which isn't well supported by game devs. And when the Pixels launched, there were reports  of compatibility issues and glitches while gaming.   The issues we've had have all seemed  to have been ironed out post-launch,   but the Galaxy remains the sure winner for gaming. At least the Pixel does a solid job  when it comes to thermal management.   We observed heavier throttling on the  S26 Ultra in our prolonged stress test. And the software experience of these two  phones feels just as snappy either way.   You get Android 17 on either device, but you'll  see a different design language on either phone,   and Samsung's One UI offers  more extensive customization. But the Pixel and the Galaxy both offer their  own takes on the same software features,   like Samsung's DeX and Pixel's desktop mode,   which both allow you to connect  the phone to an external display. Even the onboard AI-based features are  similar. Many were actually developed   in collaboration between Google and Samsung. So, while the Galaxy is better for gaming, both  phones have a comparable software experience. But which one would last longer through the day?   The Pixel 11 Pro XL has a larger  battery than the S26 Ultra. So,   with the less powerful Tensor, you'd think  it'd have the upper hand in battery life. However, the Galaxy holds its  own, and in our battery life test,   the two phones' overall active  use scores are neck and neck. There are some differences, though. The  Pixel offers longer web browsing endurance,   while the Galaxy did better in the call test. The S26 Ultra looks to have a more  substantial advantage in wired charging,   though, at least going by its 60 W rating  compared to 45 W on the Pixel 11 Pro XL. And indeed, in our charging tests, the  Galaxy was ahead after half an hour,   reaching 84% compared to the Pixel's 78%. More importantly, it took just 43 minutes for  a full charge, 17 minutes less than the Pixel. Both phones support magnetic  Qi2 wireless charging,   but you need a special case on the  Galaxy to enable the magnetic part. So far, the Galaxy has made a convincing  case for itself. It's lighter, more powerful,   faster to charge, and offers quite a  few features that the Pixel doesn't. So, if the Pixel is going to justify its price,  photography is where it needs to make its case. The Galaxy has four rear cameras compared  to the Pixel's three. But, as we'll see,   having more cameras doesn't  always mean taking better photos. The Pixel's triple camera setup  includes a 50-megapixel main cam,   a 48-megapixel 5x telephoto zoom,  and a 48-megapixel ultrawide. Meanwhile, the S26 Ultra has a 200-megapixel  main cam, a 50-megapixel 5x telephoto zoom, and   a 50-megapixel ultrawide. And filling in the gaps  at 3x zoom is another telephoto at 10 megapixels. Let's start our comparison with the still  photos. Could the Pixel make a turnaround here? Well, during the day, both phones' main cameras   do a great job. The Pixel's colors  aren't as saturated, but it actually   has a slight advantage in detail over  the higher-res camera of the Galaxy. But when using 2x digital zoom,  the Galaxy is clearly better. And here's a surprise. At 3x zoom,  you'd be hard-pressed to give the   Samsung a clear victory despite  its dedicated telephoto camera.   The Pixel with its digital zoom  is remarkably competitive here. Comparing the 5x telephoto cameras,  the results are good either way and   comparable. The Galaxy's contrast  and sharpening are tuned up a notch,   but we prefer its take, especially  where foliage is concerned. 10x zoom results are mixed. It seems  the Galaxy does better in well-lit   outdoor scenes, but the Pixel is a  winner indoors with dimmer lighting. And comparing the ultrawide cameras, we  get similar results, just with slightly   different processing. It's a matter of preference. When shooting at night, the Pixel tends  to have a yellowish white balance,   while the Galaxy's is more neutral. Both phones  have comparable levels of detail, though. It's often a toss-up for which comes out on top,   depending on the specific  scene and shooting conditions. The exception is with the ultrawide  camera at night, where the Galaxy has   a clear advantage. For whatever reason,  the Pixel is consistently weaker here. Let's look at selfies. And  your preference may depend   on whether you'd rather have the  wider field of view of the Pixel   or just have the Galaxy's higher overall  quality. You get autofocus either way. So, the Pixel has put up a surprisingly  strong fight in photography despite   having fewer rear cameras, but photos are  only half the story. How about videos? The Galaxy's video recording features are  actually quite a bit more expansive than   the Pixel's. The S26 Ultra has  a fairly decent Pro video mode,   while the Pixel doesn't go much further  than a point-and-shoot experience. The Pixel needs to resort to cloud-based  processing to give you 8K video,   and it doesn't record 4K 120 fps slow-mo. The  Galaxy will happily do both of these on-device. The Galaxy also features support  for a pro-grade video codec, APV.   And there's the Horizon Lock mode, which keeps the  orientation stable even when rotating the phone. The Galaxy is just a lot more versatile overall   for video creators. But how  does the quality compare? At 1x zoom with the main cameras,  things are nearly identical,   though the Galaxy's higher contrast  might be giving it an edge. Notably, while the Pixel was doing  just fine in stills at 3x zoom,   it can't match the Galaxy's 3x footage,  even though that is relatively soft. The Galaxy has a bit of an advantage at  5x zoom and is notably better at 10x,   but the Pixel has a slight edge in sharpness  when looking at the ultrawide videos. And here's a look at 4K selfie  videos taken with either phone. In low light, the Galaxy's  videos contain plenty of noise,   but they're still generally sharper  than the Pixel's across the board. Still, the Pixel is doing all right, except for  the ultrawide, which is best avoided at night. So, there you have it, guys.  Google's Pixel 11 Pro XL and   Samsung's S26 Ultra. Let's do a quick recap. Both phones are sturdy and well-built  with solid ingress protection. They have   premium displays with a large viewing  area. The speakers are just as loud, and the quality is good either way. Battery  life is comparable, and you get the latest   Android software experience with comparable  AI features and long software support. However, the Pixel 11 Pro XL has a  brighter display and better thermals.   There are built-in magnets for wireless charging.  Selfies are wider, and the two phones trade blows   when it comes to photo quality, even though  the Pixel lacks one of the Galaxy's cameras. The S26 Ultra, on the other hand,  is lighter and yet has a larger,   tougher, anti-reflective screen with stylus  support and a built-in privacy feature. The Galaxy also has a much more powerful  chipset. The wired charging is faster.   Its video recording capabilities  are more versatile and extensive,   and depending on where you shop, the S26  Ultra can even be the less expensive phone. In the end, the Pixel's cameras did put up  a good fight, even though they're fewer in   number, and at some zoom levels, the  Pixel even outperforms the Samsung. But the Galaxy S26 Ultra is a phone that  brings a lot more to the table. It packs   more unique features, especially with  its screen, and wins decisively when   it comes to performance, charging,  and video recording capabilities. And on top of that, in some markets, getting  the S26 Ultra can even save you some cash. So, unless you specifically prefer  Google's approach to photography   and software, the S26 Ultra is the better choice. Thanks for watching, and  I'll see you on the next one.
