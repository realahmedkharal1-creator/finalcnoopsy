Apple iPhone 18 Pro vs. iPhone 17 Pro

Hey, what's up, guys? Today we've got  two Apple flagships which might be on   your wish list. The new iPhone  18 Pro and last year's iPhone 17 Pro. These iPhones might not seem  so different, but in our testing,   we found some nuances you might be curious  about before you buy. So, let's get into it. These two iPhones look  incredibly similar. In fact,   besides the different colors,  they're hard to tell apart.   That's because the iPhone 18 Pro continues the  design language introduced with last year's model. They're both made of the same materials,  and the weight is about the same,   too. The difference is just 5 g.  Even the dimensions are identical   to the point where you can use a case  from the old model on the new one. The ingress protection is solid on both devices.  They're IP68-rated against dust and water   submersion to a depth of 6 m. It's been like that  for ages with the iPhone, so that's no surprise. And the physical controls on  the phones haven't changed. The   new model has both the Action key for  shortcuts and the Camera Control key. Just as before, Apple has also  brought over the same display to   the new model. For the most part,  both the iPhone 18 Pro and 17 Pro have 6.3-inch OLED panels with a high  resolution and a 120 Hz refresh rate. But the iPhone 18 Pro does bring a bit of  an upgrade. The new model has a smaller cutout for the front-facing cameras, so it  effectively has a larger usable screen. Apple   achieved this smaller cutout by moving the  front-facing IR camera to under the display. This didn't affect Face ID, though. It's  just as responsive as ever on the new model. And other than that cutout, the performance  of these displays is the same. In our testing,   we measured the same great maximum  brightness on both models, and both of them have an anti-reflective coating on  top to help reduce reflections and glare. So,   the bottom line is the sunlight  legibility is just as great either way. Now, to audio. The iPhone 18 Pro is louder  with a score of Good on our charts versus   Average with last year's model. The  overall quality is great either way,   though, with similar tuning. You can listen for  yourself with our comparison tool linked below. Now, the chipset, and it's one of the major  differences between these two phones. When   it comes to performance, the new iPhone  has the advantage with this new chipset, the Apple A20 Pro. It's of course meant  to pack more punch than the Apple A19 Pro   that's inside the iPhone 17 Pro. Not that  people had much to complain about there. In benchmarks, we do see a difference.  The new model outperforms the old one   by a significant amount, both in terms  of CPU scores and graphics-based ones. But even though these gains are far from trivial,  most people probably wouldn't notice a difference   in their day-to-day use cases. Both phones are  quick and snappy and handle heavy games just fine. But when we measured the thermals and  sustained performance, the new model was   slightly worse than the previous generation,  throttling lower during our stress tests. We   wouldn't call it terrible, but a bit  of a step back compared to the 17 Pro. These two phones have about the same battery  capacity. In the SIM-enabled versions,   the difference amounts to about 50 mAh  out of 4,000. But despite that negligible   difference in capacity, there was a  measurable difference in battery life. The iPhone 18 Pro showed a bit of an improvement  in our battery life scores, doing better in   screen-on tests and earning a higher active  use score, but the difference wasn't that huge. So then, how about the charging? Apple  has increased the wired charging rating   on the new model. It's gone from 35 watts to 45 W. When we measured the charging speed, the 18 Pro  is a little faster, to the tune of charging a   handful of percentage points more in a half hour  and shaving about 15 minutes off of the time to a full charge. Not a crazy difference,  but big enough that you might notice it. The Qi2 wireless charging hasn't changed,  though. On both iPhones, it's rated at 25 W. The iPhone 18 Pro can come with onboard storage  at up to 2 TB. The 17 Pro is at 1 TB instead. However, the 18 Pro's 1 and 2  TB versions use QLC storage,   which has been found to suffer a drop in write  speeds once the storage is more than 60% full. As far as software goes, there's  no surprise here. Both iPhones   run Apple's new iOS 27 with the  latest aesthetics and features. There is a difference in the camera interface,  though. On the 18 Pro, you get the option   to manually set shutter speed and white  balance. You don't get that on the old model. Speaking of the cameras, let's get into  those. The 18 Pro's main cam has a new   brighter lens with a variable aperture  that you can control. A benefit of this   is that you can manipulate the depth  of field behind a close-up subject. Background blur aside, on the 18 Pro, we didn't  really see much benefit in photo sharpness or   quality when the aperture was further opened  or closed. So, it's not really something you'd need to play with very often. You'd be good just  leaving the aperture alone and shooting as before. The iPhone 18 Pro's rear setup  overall includes that main camera,   a 4x telephoto zoom and an ultrawide, all  at 48 megapixels. And the iPhone 17 Pro has the same setup, actually identical  except for its main camera's lens. Now, let's compare the quality. During the day,   the main camera's photos are very similar.  That said, the new iPhone has a tendency   for warmer colors. It also seems to  have slightly better-defined detail. At 2x digital zoom, the new model's shots  can come out a bit sharper in some scenes. At 4x zoom, the 18 Pro continues with the  warmer colors. We wouldn't say there's   much of a difference in sharpness or detail.  And at 8x zoom, it's a bit more of the same. Comparing photos from the ultrawides,  there's not much difference we can spot   besides the warmth of the colors on the 18 Pro. In the dark, the iPhone 18 Pro's main  camera should have the advantage with   this brighter lens, but we didn't see much  of any improvement over the previous model. The 4x zoomed images at night are quite similar  between these two phones, and neither is that   sharp. The two ultrawides capture more  or less the same photos at night as well. Since last year, there have been no  changes to the selfie camera hardware,   and the selfies you'd be getting  from both phones are the same.   Great either way. And here's a look at  4K selfie videos taken with either phone. Both of these iPhones have the  same video recording capabilities,   too. When shooting during the day, quality-wise,  we struggled to spot a meaningful difference   between the footage of these two phones. The  clips are consistent and as good as identical. In a similar fashion, the nighttime videos are a   toss-up. You can hardly tell the videos  have been captured by different phones. So, there you have it. The iPhone 18  Pro versus last year's iPhone 17 Pro. Aside from the difference in colors,  you get the same design with premium   materials. The displays are sharp and  bright with an anti-reflective coating and adaptive refresh rate. The Apple  software experience is just the same,   and the results from the cameras  are basically the same, too. But the iPhone 18 Pro packs a few upgrades like  the more powerful chipset, smaller display cutout, and the louder speakers. Battery life is a bit  better, and the charging is a little faster. And   there are additional controls when shooting with  the cameras, including the variable aperture. The iPhone 17 Pro provides a very similar  user experience, nearly identical. It even   demonstrated better thermals, and since  it's from last year, the price is lower. In all, the upgrades that the iPhone  18 Pro brings compared to last year   are measurable, but not necessarily  that noticeable to the average user. And this means that in Europe, where the  €1,500 18 Pro costs €300 more than the 17 Pro,   the latter is the better deal since  it will get you 95% of the way there. But in the US, the 17 Pro seems to be discontinued  already, and the price of going with a refurbished   one is not particularly attractive. So, if you  reside there, it makes sense to go for the 18 Pro. Thanks for watching, and  I'll see you on the next one.
