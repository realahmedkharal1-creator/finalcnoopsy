Google Pixel 11 Pro vs Pixel 11 Pro XL

Pixel 11 Pro or Pixel 11 Pro XL.  Google wants you to pay around 200 bucks more for the larger model. But here's  the thing. Both phones have the same cameras, chipset, and software experience. So, is the  smaller Pixel 11 Pro actually the smarter   buy? We've run both phones through all of our  usual tests to find out. Let's get into it. The first thing you're paying for  with the XL is, of course, the size,   but bigger isn't necessarily better. The  Pixel 11 Pro is more compact and over 20   g lighter than the XL. That makes it  a bit comfier and easier to wield. But apart from size, the two phones look and feel  pretty much identical. You get the same design and   premium build materials. Even the color  options are the same. There's Canyon,   Olive, Fog, and the only matte one, Obsidian. Both phones also sport the new Highlight  feature where the LED on the back glows   for certain scenarios, like  when interacting with Gemini.   It's a neat touch, though we found it more of  a gimmick than a particularly useful feature. And both phones have IP68-rated ingress  protection against dust and water. But the extra size of the XL  comes with one obvious benefit,   a bigger screen. The Pixel 11 Pro has a 6.3-inch  OLED display, while the XL stretches that to 6.8 inches. So, if you watch a  lot of videos, play games,   or simply prefer having more screen real  estate, the XL has a clear advantage. But does the larger model have  a better-quality display, too?   Not really. Both panels are sharp and  vibrant, and our brightness tests put them   virtually neck and neck. Both get impressively  bright, making them great for outdoor use. You get a smooth high refresh rate either  way, and it's quite adaptive thanks to LTPO   tech. And under the display of  either Pixel sits an ultrasonic   fingerprint reader for biometrics,  and they're just as responsive. But the XL does have another advantage  when it comes to enjoying media.   And this one you can actually hear. The XL's  speakers are in fact louder than the Pro's, with a score of Very Good on our loudness  charts. And the sound quality is better, too,   clearer and with better bass. You can listen for  yourself with our comparison tool linked below. All right. So far, the XL gives you a bigger  screen and better audio. Fair enough. But what   about battery life? Surely a bigger phone with a  bigger battery should last considerably longer. The XL packs a 5,115 mAh battery  compared to 4,850 mAh on the smaller Pro.   It's only about 5% more capacity, though. That extra capacity didn't translate  into much longer battery life,   but the XL did come out slightly ahead in  our tests. The only meaningful difference   was in web browsing. Everywhere else,  the results were remarkably close. So, if you're expecting a major endurance  upgrade with the XL, you won't find one here. But there's a twist. The XL  may not last much longer,   but it charges a lot faster. While the  Pixel 11 Pro supports 30 W wired charging,   the XL's is 45 W. And the difference  in our test was substantial. After just half an hour, the XL would charge to  significantly more than the smaller Pro. And a   full charge took 20 minutes less on the XL. So,  while you won't get dramatically longer battery   life from the bigger phone, you will spend  noticeably less time waiting for it to charge. Wireless charging, on the other hand,  is identical. Both phones support 25   W Qi2 magnetic wireless charging,  much like Apple's MagSafe. Now, the XL is pulling ahead in a few areas, but  paying more won't get you any extra processing   power. Both phones use exactly the same Tensor G6  chipset paired with either 12 or 16 gigs of RAM. Google's Tensor chips have never  really been performance champions,   but at least the Tensor G6 promises better  CPU and AI processing power than last year. The GPU architecture, however, doesn't support  the ray tracing feature, which makes lighting and   reflections look hyper-realistic. And at launch,  it wasn't well supported by Android game devs, leading to issues in games that prompted  some pretty bad early reactions.   But since then, the problems have  generally been ironed out by Google. As expected, in benchmarks, both of  these phones earn the same scores.   It falls behind other top flagships when it comes  to graphics, but CPU-wise, it's still solid. And the larger chassis doesn't give the XL a  meaningful advantage in sustained performance   either. Both phones show similar levels of  throttling in our prolonged stress tests. So, whether you choose the Pro or the XL,   you're getting essentially the same  performance, for better or worse. The similarities continue with  storage options, which range from 256   gigs all the way up to a terabyte. And either way, you'll get  Google's software experience,   a clean yet customized version of Android 17.  It runs quite smoothly, too. Quick and snappy. But there's one more area where Google could have  done something to justify the XL's higher price,   the cameras. But instead, they haven't  given the XL any special treatment at all. Both phones have the exact same camera hardware.   That includes a main cam, a 5x telephoto zoom,  and an ultrawide camera which has autofocus. And our side-by-side testing confirms that  image quality is essentially identical, too.   So, rather than look for differences  that aren't really there, let's see   what kind of photos and videos  you can expect from either Pixel. The main camera's photos are  pretty great during the day,   with some of that characteristic Pixel  greenness, but also plenty of detail.   Dynamic range is excellent, colors are superb,  and overall there's very little to complain about. At 2x zoom, though, things aren't  quite as impressive. Google is still   lagging behind some rivals when it  comes to detail at this zoom level. Switch to the dedicated 5x  camera and things improve.   Photos are sharp, detailed,  and very pleasing overall. The ultrawides also deliver solid results,   and thanks to autofocus, you can  get up close to your subjects. At night, both Pixels remain  excellent with their main cameras,   capturing well-exposed and detailed images.   The 5x telephotos also hold up well, although  they're not quite the sharpest in the business. And the ultrawides produce decent nighttime  photos without really standing out. Selfies from the Pixels' 10.5-megapixel  front-facing cameras are great,   and we appreciate the extra-wide  field of view and autofocus here.   And here's how 4K selfie  video looks from both phones. Both phones have the same video  recording capabilities on board. As   expected, you can shoot in up to  4K at 60 fps with all cameras. Daytime footage is very good, with  attractive colors and consistent   rendering across the cameras.  Though, if you look closely,   you might notice that the detail isn't quite as  sharp as on some other leading camera phones. At night, the main cameras perform well,  while the telephotos are just decent.   The ultrawides, however,  leave a lot to be desired. So, after all of those comparisons, the  takeaway is simple. You're not getting   a better camera phone by paying extra for the XL. And that brings us back to our original question.  Is the Pixel 11 Pro XL really worth the premium? Well, there are plenty of things these two  phones share, like the same premium build quality   and equally bright and smooth displays. The  two phones have the same chipset and gaming performance. There's the same Google software  experience and exactly the same cameras. The smaller Pixel 11 Pro, however, is easier  to handle, noticeably lighter, and gives   you pretty much the same core experience,  all while saving you a fair bit of money. The XL, on the other hand, gives you a more  spacious screen, louder and fuller-sounding   speakers, and slightly longer battery life,  particularly for web browsing and social   networks. But its biggest practical advantage  may actually be the faster wired charging. So, are these improvements  worth an extra 200 bucks?   Well, if you want the biggest screen and value  faster charging, the XL certainly makes sense. But for most people, I think  the regular Pixel 11 Pro is a   smarter deal. You're not sacrificing  camera quality, performance, or any   of Google's flagship software features, and  you're saving a pretty good chunk of money. Ultimately, the XL does give you more phone,  but not necessarily 200 bucks more value. Which one would you choose? Let  us know down in the comments.
